# Stage 2: The Founder

_Expands [14-game-stages.md](../14-game-stages.md) section 14.3. Rules for founding paths, control, boards and trusts are in [detailed/1-corporate-core.md](../detailed/1-corporate-core.md); this file designs what it feels like to play the stage and the systems that only matter here. All numbers are tuning values._

## 2.1 The fantasy

You have one company, not enough money, and too many people who want a piece of it. Every week you choose between building the product, chasing customers, raising money and keeping your family and investors happy, and you never have the hours to do all four. The stage is *The Social Network* and *Silicon Valley* with a cyberpunk edge: implants to work 20-hour days, data bought from people you would rather not know, and a father whose friends own a piece of your dream.

**The core question:** can you build something that survives without losing control of it?

## 2.2 Entering the stage

| Entry | How | Starting position |
|---|---|---|
| **Found a company** (main path) | Complete a founding path chain from Stage 1 | Depends on path (see 2.4) |
| **Buy a struggling company** | Buy a small Venture or Company that is losing money | You own control but inherit its team, debts and reputation |
| **Take over the family's side business** | Family vote gives you a small trust-owned company to run | Trust is your majority holder; the dynasty head watches closely |
| **Serial founder** | Re-enter after a previous company was sold or failed | Renown and contacts from the last run, and the scars |

## 2.3 What the player tracks

The Founder stage adds a **startup dashboard** to the company screen. These seven numbers drive the stage:

| Metric | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Runway** | Months of cash left at the current burn | Zero runway means missed payroll, then bankruptcy |
| **Burn** | Monthly cash out minus cash in | Hiring and marketing raise it |
| **Product** | Product readiness, 0 to 100, through the product phases (2.5) | Gates which customers you can win |
| **Traction** | Customers and monthly revenue | What VCs, banks and acquirers look at |
| **Team** | Headcount, morale (0 to 100) and key hires | Drives product and sales progress |
| **Control** | Founder's voting share, direct and through their bloc | Below 50% you can be replaced |
| **Hype** | Market buzz, 0 to 100 | Raises valuation and hiring odds; crashes hard when promises fail |

## 2.4 How the founding path shapes the stage

The path chosen in Stage 1 sets the stage's difficulty and its main threat.

| Path | Starting cash | Control | Main pressure | Main threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Venture Capital | High | 75% to 90% after the first round, falling each round | Milestones every 12 to 24 months | VC faction removes you |
| Ask Daddy | Medium | 60% to 80%, investors are in your bloc | Favors owed, your father's reputation | Dad's friends calling in favors, family drama |
| Bootstrap | Low | 100% | Runway | Running out of cash before traction |
| Bank Loan | Medium | 100% | Monthly payments and covenants | Covenant breach, losing the collateral |
| Freestyle | Varies | Varies | A mix | Many small holders who all want something |
| Political Connections | Medium to high | 70% to 90%, government holds a golden share or seat | Government contract terms | Political change, public scrutiny |

## 2.5 Building the product

Every company moves through product phases. Each phase needs **product points** (PP).

| Phase | PP needed | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | 0 | Pitching, angel money |
| **Prototype** | 100 | Pre-seed and seed interest, demo days |
| **MVP** | 300 | First customers, pilot contracts |
| **Launch** | 600 | General sales, marketing campaigns |
| **Product-market fit** | Traction test (2.6) | Series A and up, bank credit lines, real valuation |
| **Scale** | 1,500 | Exit to Stage 3 possible |

**Product points per week** = sum over product staff of (skill / 5) + founder's Tech / 3 if the founder spends at least 20 hours that week on product + tech bonuses. Morale below 40 halves it.

**Pivot** (decision, any time before product-market fit): keep 50% of product points, change sector or customer, Hype -20, investor opinion -10 unless the pivot follows a failed launch (then +5).

## 2.6 Traction and product-market fit

- **Customers** come from sales and marketing actions, scaled by product phase.
- **Product-market fit** is reached when monthly revenue grows at least 10% a month for 3 months in a row and churn is under 5%. It is announced as an event and gives Hype +25.
- **Churn** rises when product quality lags customer count (too much growth, too few engineers).

Go-to-market channels:

| Channel | Founder time | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise sales | High (meetings) | Low | B2B sectors, slow but sticky |
| Consumer marketing | Low | High (ad spend) | Consumer apps, fast and leaky |
| Government contracts | Medium | Low | Security, infrastructure, Political Connections path |
| Partnerships | Medium | Revenue share | Riding a bigger company's reach, at the risk of dependency |
| Grey-market growth | Low | Medium | Bought data, bot armies, scraped users; fast, with exposure risk (2.12) |

## 2.7 Team

### 2.7.1 Hiring

Hiring draws from the home hub's talent pool. Roles: **Engineer**, **Sales**, **Operations**, **Executive**.

- Generic staff are numbers (headcount, average skill, salary).
- **Key hires** are characters: CTO, Head of Sales, COO, first board members. They have traits, opinions and ambitions, and they can be poached, can scheme, and can sit on your board.
- Hire chance for a key hire = 30% + Hype / 2 + salary offer above market (1% per 1%) + equity offer + opinion of the founder.

### 2.7.2 Equity and the option pool

- Every funding round may require an **option pool** (5% to 15%), which dilutes the founders before the new money comes in.
- Key hires ask for equity: 0.5% to 5% depending on role and stage.
- **Vesting**: 4 years with a 1-year cliff. A key hire who leaves before the cliff gets nothing; after it, they keep vested shares and become a small holder, possibly in a rival's bloc.

### 2.7.3 Morale

Morale (0 to 100) moves monthly:
- Up with: raises, wins (launch, funding round, big customer), a charismatic founder (Influence), equity.
- Down with: crunch, layoffs, missed payroll, founder scandals, a toxic key hire.
- Below 30: staff quit (5% of headcount a month); key hires start looking.

### 2.7.4 Co-founders

When founding, the player may invite a co-founder (a friend, sibling or classmate).
- **Equity split** is chosen at founding. An equal split keeps opinion high but means neither of you has majority control alone.
- Co-founders get a **co-founder tension** meter, raised by disagreements in decisions they care about (traits), lowered by success and shared credit.
- At high tension: a **co-founder breakup** chain. Options are buy them out, push them out with a board vote, or split the company.

## 2.8 The founder's week

Each in-game hour is a turn, but the Founder stage is designed around the **week**. The founder has about 112 waking hours a week (16 a day), 126 if Workaholic, more with stimulant implants (at a stress cost).

A typical week's time budget:

| Activity | Typical hours | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Product work | 20 to 40 | Product points |
| Customer and sales meetings | 10 to 30 | Customers, pilots |
| Fundraising | 0 to 40 (spikes during a round) | Pitch meetings and term sheets |
| Team and hiring | 5 to 15 | Hires, morale |
| Board and investors | 2 to 10 | Opinion of board and investors |
| Family | 4 to 12 | Opinion of the dynasty head and family; skipping costs opinion |
| Rest | The rest | Stress relief |

The UI offers a **weekly plan**: the player sets a time split, and auto-advance runs the hours, stopping for events and meetings. Players who want control can still play hour by hour.

**Crunch**: working more than 90 hours a week adds +10 stress per week and lowers health; a burnout event becomes likely after 4 weeks.

## 2.9 Money during the stage

Beyond the founding paths, the Founder stage offers:

| Option | When | Terms | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Next VC round | Milestone met | See detailed design | Dilution, more VC seats |
| Bridge note | Between rounds | Converts to shares at a 20% discount at the next round | Cheap now, dilutes later |
| Revenue-based financing | Revenue over $50k a month | Repay 5% to 10% of revenue until 1.5x is repaid | Squeezes cash flow |
| Strategic investor | Product phase MVP or later | A bigger company buys 10% to 30% | They want a board seat and right of first refusal on a sale |
| Family trust investment | Family vote | Trust buys a stake | Family gets a say; failure hurts your standing with them |
| Government grant | Influence in home jurisdiction | Free money with reporting rules | Scrutiny, golden share |
| Personal loan or mortgage | Any time | Uses your personal assets | Your house is on the line |

**Valuation** in this stage = traction multiple x revenue, or for pre-revenue companies, product phase base value x (1 + Hype / 100) x sector appetite.

## 2.10 Protecting control

Control is the stage's central tension. The player's tools:

- **Dual-class shares** at founding: keep class A (10 votes) and sell class B. Investors accept this less often (VC interest -15%) but it holds.
- **Bloc building**: invite friendly holders (Ask Daddy investors, family, loyal key hires) into your bloc.
- **Protective provisions**: negotiate them out of term sheets (Influence roll).
- **Founder-friendly investors**: some VC partners have a "founder-friendly" trait and rarely join factions.
- **Buybacks**: buy back shares from leaving employees and angels.

A **control meter** on the dashboard shows voting share and how far each faction is from 50%.

## 2.11 Rivals

The stage has two kinds of rivals:

- **Rival startups** (AI-run) in the same sector and hub. They compete for the same customers, staff and VC money. They can be outbuilt, poached from, sabotaged, or merged with.
- **Incumbents**: large companies that notice you once your traction passes a threshold. An incumbent picks one response: **ignore**, **copy** (launches a clone, your growth halves for 6 months unless your product is ahead), **acquire** (an offer, see 2.13), or **crush** (lawsuits, price war, lobbying against you).

## 2.12 Cyberpunk options

| Option | Effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Productivity implant | +20 founder hours a week | Humanity loss, rejection events |
| Neural focus chip for staff | +15% product points | Staff morale -10, a scandal if leaked |
| Bought user data | +30% customers from marketing | Exposure; a regulator fine or a data-leak scandal |
| Bot armies | +20 Hype | Exposure; Hype collapses to 0 if revealed |
| Netrun a rival | Steal their roadmap (+product points) or crash their launch | Discovery: lawsuit, criminal probe |
| Gig-worker labor | Ops costs -30% | Unrest in the hub, union events |

Each use adds **exposure** to the company. Exposure feeds the same investigation rules as shell companies.

## 2.13 Exits

### 2.13.1 Success: move to Stage 3

Requirements: product phase **Scale**, company rank **Company** or higher, and the company founds or buys its **first vassal**. A "Coming of Age" event marks the move to Stage 3.

### 2.13.2 Sell the company

An acquirer (incumbent, strategic investor, or rival dynasty) makes an offer at any time after MVP. Selling gives cash to every holder by their stake. The founder can:
- Take the cash and leave (move to Stage 1-like play as a wealthy **serial founder** or **angel investor**, with renown +50).
- Stay on as a vassal CEO under the buyer (becomes a character inside someone else's company, with a stake and a grudge or a loyalty).

### 2.13.3 Failure

| Failure | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Bankruptcy** | Missed payroll twice, or debt default | Company liquidated; personal guarantees called; reputation -30; Dad's opinion -30 if Ask Daddy |
| **Acquihire** | Runway under 3 months and an offer arrives | Team joins a buyer; investors get little; founder gets a job and a small payout |
| **Ousted** | A faction wins a proxy fight or the board votes you out | You keep your shares as a minority holder; reputation -10; you can found again or try to win the company back |
| **Fire sale** | Founder chooses to sell under distress | Low price, reputation -10, but no bankruptcy record |

Failure is not game over: the dynasty and trust continue. A founder who fails young is a common CK3-style story and can come back stronger.

## 2.14 Family pressure

The founder is still a member of a dynasty, and the family does not stop:

- The dynasty head's opinion of you rises with success (+5 per milestone) and falls with scandals and failure.
- **Family obligations**: weddings, funerals, holidays and trust meetings are events that cost hours. Skipping them costs opinion.
- **Sibling rivalry**: a sibling may found a competing company, invest in a rival, or lobby the head against funding you.
- **Succession while founding**: if the dynasty head dies during this stage, the player may become trustee while also running a startup. Both demand hours.

## 2.15 Founder events (starting set)

| Event | Trigger | Options |
|---|---|---|
| Demo Day | Prototype reached | Pitch big (Influence, Hype up or down), pitch safe, skip |
| The Clone | Incumbent chooses copy | Outbuild them, sue, seek a buyer |
| Payroll Friday | Runway under 1 month | Personal loan, skip your salary, lay off 20%, ask Dad |
| The Poacher | Rival offers your CTO double | Match, counter with equity, let them go |
| Co-Founder Fight | Co-founder tension high | Talk it out, buy them out, board vote |
| First Big Customer | Enterprise deal in reach | Discount to close, hold price, custom features (product points cost) |
| Viral Moment | Random, Hype above 50 | Ride it (marketing spend), stay calm |
| The Leak | Grey-market growth exposure | Deny, apologize, blame a contractor |
| Down Round | Missed VC milestone | Accept, bridge note, fight the faction |
| Acquisition Offer | Incumbent chooses acquire | Accept, negotiate (Finance), refuse |
| Burnout | 4 weeks of crunch | Take a week off, implants, push through |
| Dad's Friend Wants a Job | Ask Daddy favor | Hire his son, refuse, find a compromise |
| Regulator Letter | Sector with regulation, traction over a threshold | Comply (cost), lobby, ignore |
| Product-Market Fit | Traction test passed | Celebration choices: raise now, grow on revenue, reward the team |
| Office Romance | Two key hires become lovers | Allow, separate teams, fire one |

## 2.16 Pacing and screens

- **Length target**: 3 to 8 in-game years. Roughly 2 to 4 hours of real play.
- **Main screens**: company dashboard (2.3), weekly planner (2.8), team panel, funding panel with cap table and control meter, character screens for key hires and investors.
- **Map** is mostly the home hub; one other hub may open through a partnership or a second office.
- **Markets and politics** stay in the background: interest rates set loan costs, and hub influence can unlock Political Connections, but there are no exchanges or elections to manage yet.

## 2.17 Example run

Marcus Vale, 23, second son of a mid-sized real estate dynasty, takes the Ask Daddy path to found a cyberware fitting clinic chain in his home hub. His father's support is 55, so dinner goes well and he meets five of his father's friends: three say yes, one says "yes, but" (wants his nephew hired), one refuses. Marcus raises $3M and keeps 72% of the votes, with the three friends in his bloc.

He spends his first months on product work and reaches MVP in month 7. In month 10 a rival startup poaches his head of fitting. In month 14 he hits product-market fit, and Hype jumps. A Series A from a VC firm brings $12M, an option pool and a VC partner on the board; Marcus is now at 58% with his bloc. The nephew turns out to be a disaster, and firing him costs 30 opinion with his father's friend, who leaves the bloc. Marcus drops to 49%: contested.

He spends two months winning back votes with a buyback from angels and recruiting his loyal CTO into the bloc, and gets back to 53%. In year 4, he founds a subsidiary to make implant components, reaches Company rank, and moves to Stage 3 as a Climber.

## 2.18 Assumptions

- The weekly planner is the main way to play this stage, with hour-by-hour play optional.
- Product phases and product-market fit apply to every sector, with sector-specific names (for example, a mine's "Prototype" is a survey and its "MVP" is a pilot pit).
- Rival startups exist only in this stage; incumbents are regular AI companies.
