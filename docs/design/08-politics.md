# 8. Politics

Politics fuses Victoria 3's interest groups and laws with EU4-style states and diplomacy, seen from the point of view of a dynasty that does not rule, but buys influence over those who do.

## 8.1 Levels of government

| Level | Examples of powers |
|---|---|
| **National** | Currency and central bank independence, foreign ownership rules, tariffs, national taxes, antitrust, securities law, sanctions, military |
| **Regional** (state or province) | Regional taxes, labor law, licensing, infrastructure |
| **Local** (city or hub) | Zoning and permits, property tax, policing, local contracts |

Each government has a **type** (liberal democracy, managed democracy, one-party state, corporate oligarchy, military junta, failed state) that decides how power changes hands and how much money and influence can buy.

## 8.2 Interest groups and parties

- **Interest groups** (Vic3 style): Industrialists, Financiers, Tech Sector, Labor Unions, Gig Workers, Landowners, Security Establishment, Religious and Traditionalists, Environmentalists, Transhumanists (pro-cyberware).
- Groups draw support from population segments (see [06-economy.md](06-economy.md)) and from money. Their **clout** decides which laws pass.
- **Parties** bundle interest groups and contest elections in democracies.

## 8.3 Laws and policies

Laws are grouped in categories, each with several positions:

- **Economy**: tax rates (income, corporate, capital gains, inheritance), tariffs, subsidies, price controls.
- **Corporate**: foreign ownership limits, branch office requirements, antitrust strictness, corporate charter rules, bankruptcy law.
- **Finance**: central bank independence, capital controls, exchange rate regime, securities regulation, banking secrecy (shell havens).
- **Labor**: minimum wage, unions, automation tax, gig worker rights.
- **Tech and body**: cyberware legality, data privacy, AI regulation, surveillance.
- **Security**: private military legality, policing model, emergency powers.

## 8.4 Elections and power transfer

- Democracies hold elections on fixed schedules (per level). Campaign funding, media ownership, ad campaigns and psyops shift results.
- Dynasty members can **run for office**, be **appointed** (ministers, regulators, central bankers), or act as **patrons** of politicians.
- Non-democracies change through appointments, internal party struggles, coups and revolutions. A dynasty with enough influence can back a faction, or a coup (with PMCs).

## 8.5 Regulators and the state

- **Regulators**: securities regulator, antitrust authority, tax authority, financial police, foreign investment review. Each is led by a character who can be lobbied, bribed or blackmailed.
- **Investigations** are event chains: subpoena, discovery, settlement or trial. Outcomes: fines, forced divestment, banned executives, prison.
- **Government contracts**: defense, infrastructure, healthcare, surveillance; won by bid plus influence.
- **Bailouts and nationalization**: governments rescue systemically important companies, often taking stakes; hostile governments seize branch offices.

## 8.6 Lobbying and political influence

Political influence is mainly rooted in assets owned in a jurisdiction (see [05-map-and-influence.md](05-map-and-influence.md)), plus offices held and favors owed. It is spent on:

- **Lobbying** for or against a law change (raises the support of a law among interest groups).
- **Campaign donations** and **super-PAC style** spending.
- **Permits, zoning and contracts** at local level.
- **Protection** from investigations.
- **Political Connections** founding path.

## 8.7 International relations

Countries are AI actors with EU4-lite diplomacy:

- **Relations**, **trade agreements**, **alliances**, **rivalries**.
- **Sanctions** cut trade routes and freeze assets of sanctioned holders; branch offices in sanctioned countries face hard choices.
- **Trade wars** raise tariffs.
- **Conflicts**: wars are background events that disrupt hubs and routes, and create demand for defense and PMCs. Players do not command armies, but companies can profit from or be ruined by them.
- **Stability**: unrest, inflation, scandal and unemployment lower stability; low stability raises the risk of revolution, default and currency crisis.

## 8.8 Corporate sovereignty (cyberpunk endgame)

At extreme influence, a dynasty or megacorp can push a hub toward **corporate sovereignty**: a charter city or special economic zone run by the company, with its own laws and security forces. This is a late-game goal and a huge source of controversy.

## 8.10 Ideology

A Victoria 2 style belief layer sits under the interest groups ([detailed/5-ideology.md](detailed/5-ideology.md)). Every population segment in every hub holds a mix of thirteen cyberpunk ideologies: Market Liberals, Social Democrats, Communists, Nationalists, Conservatives, Greens, Accelerationists, Effective Altruists, Transhumanists, Neo-Luddites, Sovereigntists, Technocrats and Crypto-Anarchists. The mix drifts with unemployment, automation, cyberware, AI, inequality, media and propaganda. Ideology feeds interest groups and party votes, grows movements and radical wings that target companies, decides what state a revolution creates, gives every character a CK3-faith-like belief, and shifts how a dynasty's brands sell.
