# 7. Markets: stocks, bonds, commodities and FX

## 7.1 Stock exchanges

Major countries have a **stock exchange** (fictional names for real-world equivalents), each with trading hours in its local time zone. Hourly turns mean markets open and close around the globe as the day passes.

- **Listing**: a company IPOs on an exchange through an event chain (bankers, roadshow, pricing). Listing raises cash and creates public float but adds disclosure rules and activist risk.
- **Price formation**: an agent-based model. Each listed stock has a fundamental value (from earnings, growth, assets) and a market price that moves hourly during trading hours toward it, pushed by:
  - order flow from AI investor types (index funds, value funds, momentum traders, retail, activists, sovereign funds),
  - news and events (earnings, scandals, takeovers, sector shocks),
  - sentiment (sector and market-wide),
  - player and AI trades (with price impact).
- **Indices**: each exchange has a headline index and sector indices; index funds buy constituents automatically.
- **Corporate actions**: secondary offerings, buybacks, stock splits, dividends, rights issues, delisting.
- **Short selling**: borrow and sell shares; short squeezes are possible.
- **Options** (later): calls and puts for hedging and speculation.

## 7.2 Bond markets

- Governments (national, regional, city) and companies issue bonds with a coupon, maturity and credit rating.
- **Credit ratings** (AAA to D) from rating agencies move with fundamentals and can be influenced (scheme).
- Yields move with central bank rates, inflation and default risk. Defaults trigger restructuring events.

## 7.3 Commodity markets

- Globally traded goods (oil, gas, metals, grain, chips, energy) have commodity exchange prices linked to the economy's supply and demand (see [06-economy.md](06-economy.md)).
- Spot and futures positions; futures let companies hedge input costs.
- Cornering a market is possible but draws regulators.

## 7.4 Foreign exchange

- Each country (or currency zone) has a **currency**. Every price, account and asset is denominated in one.
- **Exchange rates** float hourly based on trade balances, interest rate differences, capital flows, risk sentiment and central bank intervention.
- **Regimes**: free float, managed float, peg, or hard capital controls (for example China), set by each country's laws.
- **Capital controls** limit moving money in and out, which is why branch offices matter.
- Players can hold foreign currency, run carry trades, hedge with forwards, or (with enough capital and influence) attack a weak peg.
- **Currency crises**: a peg breaks, the currency collapses, foreign debt explodes, governments fall.

## 7.5 Central banks

Each currency has a central bank (AI controlled, influenced by politics):

- Sets the **policy rate**, which drives savings yields, bond yields, loan costs and FX.
- Targets inflation and employment; reacts monthly.
- Can intervene in FX, run QE or bailouts in crises.
- Independence is a law; less independent banks follow the government's wishes, and so the lobbying of whoever has influence.

## 7.6 Market regulation

Securities regulators enforce disclosure, insider trading and market manipulation rules (see [08-politics.md](08-politics.md)). Insider trading is a tempting scheme with heavy penalties if caught.

## 7.7 Phase 1 abstraction

Until Phase 3, prices update monthly from company performance plus noise, with one currency and a fixed interest rate.
