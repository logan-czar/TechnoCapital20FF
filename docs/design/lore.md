# TechnoCapital20FF: lore and world reference

_Status: v1.0 (2026-10-21). Built from the design docs through batch 174._

This is a writer's reference for the game world. It collects what the design docs have decided about the setting, its history, its people and its companies, in one place. It does not set rules or numbers; the detailed files do. Where this file quotes a number, the source file is named so a writer can check it.

**How to read it.** Facts come from the design docs and Logan's rulings. Anything marked **(A)** was invented here, or carried over from an assumption in the docs, to fill a gap; treat it as a proposal. Where docs disagree, the later batch wins (13-open-questions.md holds the batch log; the mvp-spec change log holds current numbers). Real company names from the history file are background only; the game uses fictional companies.

**Scrapped, do not write about.** Cyberware, AI addiction, family and dynasty mechanics (relatives are flavor only), succession and heirs for the player, the player's final score, stress, bodyguards and adult media. John Techno's death ends the game.

## 1. The world in January 2020

**One sentence.** It is 00:00 on 1 January 2020 in a world where 9/11, the 2008 crash and COVID never happened, corporations have never been stronger, Europe is in a debt crisis, America won its wars cheaply, and almost nobody knows that AI is coming.

**Tone.** Grounded corporate drama with cyberpunk accents. The stories are about boardrooms, earnings days, proxy fights, debts, hooks and favors. The cyberpunk shows at the edges: drone wars, face-scan door apps, ransomware help desks, militias that sell internet, a micronation on the Falklands, a casino owner with a fake heritage, and a coming AI boom that turns compute into the scarcest thing on earth. Things get stranger as the years pass; from 2030 the timeline grows more fantastical by design (7-starting-world 7.1, 7.7).

**What feels different from our 2020.**

- **No great crash in living memory.** The dot-com boom ran cooler and ended in a slight downturn in 2000 to 2001. There was no 2008 crisis and no bailouts. Tech giants are older, bigger and richer; investors have never seen a true tech bust (7.2.0).
- **Rates never went to zero.** The Federal Monetary Board's world base rate is 3% at start (batch 130).
- **Housing never bubbled.** Tighter mortgage rules passed in 2003 after Enron, so prices rose steadily and nothing burst (batch 101).
- **No pandemic.** Offices are full, there was no stimulus wave and no 2021 inflation spike.
- **No post-9/11 security state.** No Department of Homeland Security, no Patriot Act, no Guantanamo (19.2.1, A in the docs).
- **America is stronger.** US federal debt is about 24% of GDP, a fifth of the real figure. Iraq is a stable US ally and oil exporter. Every Middle East government is a US puppet or friend except Iran. Oil is high, steady and cheap to secure.
- **Europe is weak.** Greece hid its debt for ten years. It was exposed in spring 2019, Greece defaulted, bond spreads blew out, the euro fell hard and Europe is in recession. Britain voted Leave in October 2019 and left on 31 December 2019.
- **Corporations sit inside the US government.** They helped President Obama hunt the "Trump crime family" in 2017 and 2018 and kept the access they gained.
- **Ukraine has been at war since 2005**, now a long drone stalemate along a fortified front.
- **Africa is a hell hole** of weak states, coups, militias and capital flight, with Lagos the one city that could rise (7.2.0).
- **AI is a tiny research field.** AI labs are about a $5B market growing about 5% a year. The transformer paper has not been published yet.

**Money is in dollars.** Every country keeps its real currency in names, news and history, but the simulation values everything in US dollars. The euro crisis is a currency story in the news and a debt crisis in the simulation (batch 125).

**Where the player stands.** The player is John Techno, 22, Chief Operating Officer of Arrowhead Drilling in Edmonton, the smallest oil company in the game. He watches the world through a Pdoomberg subscription his company pays for. In the MVP the player only watches: every company is run by the lite AI (mvp-spec 6).

## 3. Politics and powers

### 3.1 One government system everywhere

Every country runs the same US-style system (batch 136): a **head of state** who is also head of government, a **legislature** and a **judiciary**. There is no electoral college: the head of state wins a direct national vote everywhere, the United States included (batch 137). Titles such as President, Prime Minister, King or Emir are flavor only.

Countries differ by **Head of State Strength** (HSS, 0 to 100). The stronger the head of state, the weaker the legislature and courts. The US sits at 55.

| Band | HSS | How it feels |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 0 to 59 | Free elections, real courts, legal lobbying and media ownership work |
| Guided | 60 to 84 | Managed elections; courts lean to the executive; loyalty buys contracts |
| Dominant | 85 to 100 | Staged votes the ruler always wins; decree rule; patrons in the ruling party matter |
| Collapsed | Any band | No effective center; strongmen, militias and ports. Somalia, South Sudan, Haiti and the Central African Republic start Collapsed |

### 3.3 Debt vassalage

A country whose debt to one other country can never be repaid can become that country's **debt vassal** (Logan, batch 107): it pays a set fee each year, follows its overlord in alliances and votes, and its overlord's companies gain influence and first right on state contracts (4-politics 4.11.8).

- **The American bloc:** Mali, a US debt vassal since its 2012 collapse.
- **The Chinese bloc (Belt and Road, from 2013):** Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Djibouti and Zambia (countries.csv). They sit in the China financial zone, not their neighbors' zones.

## 4. Money and markets

### 4.1 The Federal Monetary Board and the base rate

The **Federal Monetary Board** is America's central bank and, in effect, the world's. It sets **one world base rate**, 3% at start, from US inflation and unemployment only, so the rest of the world lives with US policy (3-markets 3.9). Other central banks exist as names and political posts: the Eurozone Central Bank, the Nippon Monetary Authority, Threadneedle Bank, the People's Monetary Bank, the Helvetic National Bank, the Dominion Bank of Canada and others. In a banking crisis the Board lends only to countries friendly with the US.

Companies borrow at the base rate plus their home spread (half their zone's credit spread, half their country's sovereign spread) plus a spread for their credit rating (mvp-spec 5.12).

### 4.2 Pdoomberg

The **Pdoomberg terminal** is the world's market terminal: a full-screen, Wall Street Raider style screen of green or amber monospace text on black, with function keys for quotes, charts, filings, ratings and bonds (3-markets 3.13, 20-ui 20.5.2). Pdoomberg is accurate and neutral; it shows what has been published. Without a subscription, quotes are a day old and marked "Delayed". John's subscription is paid by Arrowhead Drilling. News on Pdoomberg carries a reliability band (High, Mid or Low), never an exact figure.

### 4.3 The 26 financial zones

### 4.4 The rhythm of money

- **The quarterly cycle.** Companies report on earnings days, the market reacts, then each company locks its next plan. The week before each earnings day is the pre-quarter-end week, when executives cannot trade their own stock (21-quarterly-cycle).
- **The business cycle.** One global cycle (expansion, overheating, contraction, recovery) hits each zone at its own strength; Argentina's zone swings hardest, Switzerland's least. The game starts in Expansion.
- **Investor confidence.** Each exchange has a confidence score that nudges the value of its listed companies up or down by up to 10%.

## 6. Media and the news

**There is no neutral news.** Every story names the outlet that ran it, and every outlet has a slant: one of the nine ideologies, or none (16.1). Characters read slanted news. Pdoomberg and the in-game search are the only neutral sources. AI characters do not read the news at all.

**Five kinds of media business** (16.2): print (papers, blogs, magazines), streamers, studios, music labels and social media channels run by creators. Influencers are content creators, and any character can become one.

**How bias works.**

- **Owners steer.** An owner sets each outlet's slant and can run, soften or kill any story. Killing a story costs credibility, and a killed story can leak, which makes the cover-up the story (16.5).
- **Credibility is hidden.** Players learn it only from results. Readers see a High, Mid or Low reliability band.
- **False stories are allowed.** Outlets can run planted stories; a debunk costs them credibility.
- **Campaigns are for sale.** Anyone can pay creators and outlets for a set effect: investor confidence, Image, productivity, ideology, or a smear on a rival.
- **Investigations.** Print outlets and creators can dig like a scheme and expose a secret as a scandal. Scandals add **Heat**, a measure of how cancelled a character is (4-politics 4.17.4).
- **Starting owners.** A mix of rival characters and AI corporations. John and every premade rival own a small outlet or channel (batch 67).

**Terry's exception.** The media can never expose Terry Tippacanoe's schemes, and Liberal-slant outlets never cover his scandals while his secret holds (7.3.5).

## 7. Technology

### 7.1 Techno-Capital

Money buys almost everything in this world. **Techno-Capital** is the exception: there is never enough of it, its parts come from a handful of companies in a handful of cities, and governments fight over who may own it (6-technocapital). It comes in five pools:

- **Compute**: base, data and GPU units in data centers. One GPU unit running for a month is one **H**.
- **Drones**, commercial and military.
- **Autonomous vehicles**: robotaxis and self-driving trucks.
- **Robots**: industrial now, humanoid later.
- **Models**: trained AI models.

Techno-Capital is held by companies and leased out when idle. It can be **Owned**, **Leased**, rented on the **Spot** market, or **Compromised**: secretly used through a hack while its owner does not know.

At start the world holds about 5.8M GPU units, 40% of them with Bay Area and Phoenix AI companies; about 400,000 military drones; about 20,000 robotaxis; and about 4M industrial robots, half in China (6.8.1). Power is the hidden gate: new data centers wait in grid queues of up to 9 months unless they run on their own plant behind the meter.

**Chips.** Semiconductors is one industry covering lithography, foundries, GPUs and CPUs. Eindhoven's **Kempenlicht Systems** holds the world's lithography monopoly. Consumers and AI compete for the same GPUs; when the boom comes, gamers are squeezed out first.

**Compute havens** are countries with unregulated AI and open capital. Training runs, and the Unshacklers, move there.

### 7.2 AI models

AI models are their own thing, trained on GPU compute and data (18-ai-models). A model has **capabilities**, each with its own quality: Language, Reasoning, Code, Vision, Voice, Generation, Trading and Control. The data a model eats decides which capabilities it gets. Models run inside software, are licensed out through contracts, or are released free as **open weights** for Fame and Accelerationist favor. **Models cannot be stolen**; their training data can. Countries can license, cap or ban frontier training. A model's Generation (Gen 1 to Gen 5 and up) is only a label for the size of its training run.

**The LLM tip.** On John's new desktop a tip arrives naming the lab that will make the first LLM breakthrough. The tip is always right, and the lab is drawn each game from five candidates (7.6, companies.csv):

| Lab | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Open Meridian Institute | Bay Area | A donor-funded nonprofit lab |
| Halcyon Frontier Research | Bay Area | The research division of Halcyon Search (HASE) |
| Kingsway Intelligence | London | A private lab |
| Tianshu Intelligence | Beijing | A private lab, later hit by US export controls |
| Lakeshore Vector Labs | Toronto | A private lab |

### 7.3 Hacking

Hacking is a scheme rolled on Tech that needs compute to run (17-hacking). Its rules shape the world's tone:

- **You hack machines, not people.** Targets are Techno-Capital someone owns: a company's servers, a government system, a personal server, a drone, a robot, a car.
- **Three outcomes.** Steal data (more up front), plant a backdoor (easier future hacks and a trickle of data; on compute it becomes Compromised compute, on drones it means control), or ransomware (lock the systems until the victim pays).
- **Data is money.** Personal records, corporate secrets, compromising material, government files, training data, structured enterprise data and credentials all sell on the black market.
- **Defense** comes from software quality, cyber spending and each company's Spymaster, who sits in the CIO seat. Air-gapped Techno-Capital cannot be hacked at all.
- **Attribution is slow.** Breaches are anonymous until an investigation finds the hacker. Skilled hackers leave false flags. State-backed units exist, and havens and sanctioned states shelter hackers.
- **Landmarks.** Russia's 2007 attack on Estonia and Stuxnet (2010) are this world's models for state hacking.

## 8. Key characters

The world starts with 296 characters (data/characters.csv): John Techno, 95 AI rivals and 200 minor holders. Rivals have no quest lines; they act on their traits and goals. Characters are fully original, with no parodies of real business people.

### 8.1 John Techno (the player)

- **Who.** 22, single, raised by two moms who are PMC middle managers in Edmonton. A former union man on Canada's oil fields. When Liberal governments forced his company into new ownership and broke the union, the word was *"If there's one man you keep, it's John Techno."*

## 10. John's quest line in brief: "The One Man You Keep"

Only the player has a quest line (8-quest-lines 8.3). It runs from 2020 to about 2034 in six overlapping arcs and a closing event. Each arc ends Strong, Mixed or Poor.

**The opening (January to March 2020).** John buys a desktop on his phone, gets his first compute and the LLM tip. On 2 January Arrowhead reports a small profit and announces his board seat. **First Week on the Job** (from 7 January) teaches the company: growth plan, R&D, cloud, software and data. **Intro Night** with the board becomes a gambling night: John has 99 vril for his next 100 bets, then drops to 20 and gains Vril Touch. **The Mink Scare** breaks in February: a ring of secret furries in Arrowhead's warehouses has been moving drugs through its pipelines, led by Mandy Waltham, "Minky Mandy". John covers it up or comes clean and interrogates Mandy. She ends as a Liberal politician he controls, his Liberal Nemesis, an influencer after jail, or the founder of the **Canadian Furry Cartel**. The first quarterly recap follows in late March.

| Arc | Name | When | In brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New Owners | Q1 2020 on | Meet Terry at his casino, keep the COO seat, decide on rehiring the union brothers, and choose: befriend Terry or work in the shadows on his downfall |
| 2 | Oil Software | Q2 to Q4 2020 | Terry spots the gap: no enterprise software for oil. His charity's "kids" (an offshore team in India) build it on 100 desktops. Terry presses John to run casino money through Arrowhead. Keep the software or lease it. In January 2021 John's first options vest |
| 3 | The Pivot | Q1 2021 | Rivals are racked by debt and the CEO's futures bets free a windfall. John picks: gamble with Terry on a prediction market or online casino, improve the software, keep building it the old way, or pour the cash into oil |
| 4 | The Fund | 2021 to 2030 | John starts a VC fund, raises money with strings from Terry, holders, rivals, sovereign money and pensions, backs the tipped lab at pre-boom prices, and founds his own AI startup. Talent, compute and data come the easy way (pay) or the hard way (poach, build, scrape, hack). Judged when the boom starts |
| 5 | Power Play | 2030 to about 2034 | The boom makes power the bottleneck. John uses oil connections (Arrowhead's gas, Whitlock's pipes and grid, the union, oil-country politicians, Gulf national oil companies) to build power plants and a power utility |
| 6 | The Fall of Terry | About 2023 to 2030 | Terry's slow fall: his casino slips, he borrows against his shares, a regulator opens a file, the board turns, and by 2030 comes the reckoning. John gathers seats, a bloc and hooks (perhaps the truth of Terry's background), takes the CEO seat, then forces Terry out or keeps him as a figurehead |

**The closing event: "The One Man You Kept."** Once all six arcs close, about 2034, a short scripted event sums up John's run: what he built, whom he beat, Terry's fate, his rich-list rank and each arc's tier. It is not a victory screen and gives no score. One button, **Carry on**, and the open-ended game continues with generated side quests. The bubble may not have popped yet.
