### 19.2.1 The early war on al-Qaeda (feasibility check)

Logan asked whether the US could plausibly crush al-Qaeda after the Cole, before 9/11. **Verdict: yes, it is feasible**, because every piece below existed or was on the table in real life; the game world just acts on them. The only stretch is speed and public will: without a 9/11-scale shock there is no full invasion of Afghanistan, so the campaign is covert and by proxy, and it takes about two years.

| Step | Real life | Game world |
|---|---|---|
| Blame | The FBI and CIA had strong evidence against al-Qaeda by December 2000, but no one officially pinned it on them before Clinton left, and Bush did not respond. | Yemen cooperates quickly and the US names al-Qaeda in **November 2000**. |
| First strike | Clinton had cruise-missile submarines on station and had struck Afghan camps once before (1998). | Lame-duck Clinton strikes the Afghan training camps in **December 2000**. Bin Laden escapes. |
| The plan | Richard Clarke's plan (arm the Northern Alliance, hit the camps, squeeze Pakistan) was handed over in January 2001 and shelved until after 9/11. | Bush adopts it in **early 2001**: CIA teams and money go to Ahmad Shah Massoud's Northern Alliance, and Pakistan is pushed to cut off the Taliban. |
| Drones | The armed Predator was test-fired in February 2001 but not used before 9/11. | Armed Predators fly over Afghanistan from **summer 2001**. |
| The plot | The Cole planner Khallad bin Attash linked the CIA to the January 2000 Kuala Lumpur meeting, where two future hijackers (al-Mihdhar and al-Hazmi) were seen. They lived openly in San Diego and were not watchlisted until August 2001. | The Cole trail gets them watchlisted in **early 2001**. Their arrest, with Moussaoui's in August, unravels the plot. **9/11 never happens.** |
| Massoud | Assassinated on 9 September 2001 by al-Qaeda men posing as journalists. | With US teams beside him, the assassins are caught. **Massoud lives** **(A)**. |
| The end | (none) | The Northern Alliance, with US air power, pushes the Taliban out of Kabul in **2002**. **Bin Laden is killed by a Predator in 2003** **(A)**. Small al-Qaeda cells survive in Yemen and elsewhere, but as a global network it is broken. |

Effects **(A)**: no Department of Homeland Security, no Patriot Act, and no Guantánamo. US power in the region looks strong and cheap, which feeds into the quick Iraq war (7.2.0). Massoud becomes the big man of a US-aligned Afghanistan.
